# Beyond the Clinic (Aalto University) — main challenge

Brief as given on the Junction 2026 challenge page, copied 15 November 2026.

## The challenge
Build the product that helps people stay well at home after depression treatment, using anything from natural
stimuli to a phone, music, games, light or AI.

## Introduction
Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) is used in hospitals and clinics to treat people with severe depression.
The treatment usually requires multiple brief daily visits for brain stimulation. Even if the treatment is
successful, there is a risk that depression returns. Therefore, we would like to have "maintenance" treatment at
home.

In the hospital, you can obtain blood samples, get brain scans with fMRI, MEG or EEG, and be consulted by a
psychiatrist. At home you don't have these, except for an occasional remote psychotherapy meeting, and that is also
difficult or expensive. But brain activity and mental condition at home can be measured in several ways. No good
solution has yet been developed for maintenance treatment at home, and that is the challenge.

Home-based maintenance treatment could also be "closed-loop", meaning that it is individualised based on how the
brain of the patient reacts to the interventions. In other words, your product can work as a loop: sense how
someone is doing, respond with something that helps, and learn what works best for that person.

## Who you can design for
- People after TMS treatment, who want to keep feeling well once the daily hospital visits end.
- Anyone at risk of depression, who could benefit from early, everyday support.
- Clinicians, who want to follow how their patients are doing between visits without adding to their workload.

## Ideas to get you started
You don't need special hardware. A laptop and a phone are enough to build something great.

**What could help people stay well**
- Cleverly designed sensory stimulation (auditory, visual, touch, smell, taste), for example music or rhythmic
  lights or colours.
- Social interaction, meditation, yoga and physical exercise.
- Regular visits to forests or to the sea, or experiencing them in VR when going out is hard.
- Computer games, such as that of Soihtu.

**How to sense how someone is doing at home**
- The mobile phone can measure sounds, voice, movement (acceleration), facial expressions, sleep and daily routines.
- Wearables such as smart rings can measure heart signals (HRV), sleep, activity and changes in the autonomic
  nervous system, a very important indicator of mental state.
- Many other sensors and technologies exist, or can be invented.

**Product directions we'd love to see**
- Everyday companion: a phone or AI check-in that notices changes over time and suggests the right thing at the
  right moment.
- Sensory experience: music, light or VR that adapts to how the person is doing that day.
- Game: something fun to play daily that also supports mood and wellbeing.
- Smart home: lights and sound that gently change through the day.
- Social: a way for friends or peers to stay close and reach out when it matters.
- Clinician view: a simple way for professionals to see who might need a call.

For inspiration, look at Meru Health and other companies developing ways to alleviate depression.

## Mentors and resources
- Researchers from a group at Aalto's Department of Neuroscience and Biomedical Engineering, plus support from
  Aalto Innovation.
- Published research and open data: pointers to published studies and openly available datasets.
- Expert mentors who know brain stimulation, depression treatment and how the brain can be measured.
- Demos, subject to confirmation with the lab: a live TMS demo; a portable EEG demo at the booth.

## Judging criteria
| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Usefulness | 25% | Could it really help people stay well at home? |
| Ease of use | 25% | Would someone recovering from depression actually use it every day? |
| Smart sensing | 25% | Does it make clever use of what can be measured at home, and learn what works for each person? |
| Creativity | 25% | How original and delightful is the idea? |

## Prizes
2000 € for the best team; a visit to the brain stimulation lab; for the most promising idea, a chance to keep
developing it together with the researchers.
